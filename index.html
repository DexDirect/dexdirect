Abstract

Scalable collection of dexterous manipulation demonstrations remains a major bottleneck for robot learning. High-fidelity interfaces often require costly hardware and extensive setup, while low-setup, low-cost alternatives tend to provide less precise control and impose greater cognitive workload on operators. We present DexDirect, a direct kinesthetic arm guidance for efficient dexterous demonstration collection. The operator drags a 6-DoF gravity-compensated robot arm directly by a handle, while a single webcam retargets operator’s other hand onto a 16 joints 13-DoF dexterous robot hand. User studies suggest DexDirect collects 17.2× and 3.2× more successful demonstrations compared to purely vision (AnyTeleop) and pose-tracking (TeleDex) baselines. An adapted NASA-TLX shows DexDirect greatly reduces mental demand, effort, and frustration, despite raising physical demand. A diffusion policy trained on DexDirect demonstrations reaches a 90% success rate on a cube pick-and-place task. These results suggest that direct kinesthetic arm guidance combined with vision-based hand retargeting provides an efficient low-setup and scalable interface for collecting dexterous manipulation demonstrations.

Two Hands. Two Pathways.

Arm motion needs global 6-DoF positioning and physical contact. Finger articulation is high-dimensional but lives in a hand-local frame. DexDirect gives each the interface that suits it, with no leader arm, no operator-to-robot calibration and no arm-side retargeting.

Left hand

Drag the Arm

The arm runs in a gravity-compensated drag mode with zero position stiffness and low joint damping. A staged system identification corrects the model gravity torques for payload, torque scaling and friction. Trajectories come straight from the joint encoders, with no pose estimation, retargeting or IK, and the rigid handle lets the operator feel contact.

Webcam the Fingers

A palm-facing webcam feeds MediaPipe Hands, and wrist-relative landmarks are mapped to the MIDAS hand with the dex-retargeting framework released with AnyTeleop. The camera never estimates the wrist pose because the arm already sets it. A 3D-printed forearm support keeps the palm centered in view.

More Demonstrations. Less Workload.

Lower Workload

On an adapted NASA-TLX, DexDirect’s overall Raw TLX score was 2.01, against 3.08 for TeleDex and 3.81 for AnyTeleop (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank, p = 0.0195 and p = 0.00195).

Limitations

Co-located by design

Needs a robot that supports safe, low-impedance gravity-compensated guidance, does not support remote teleoperation, and may be less ergonomic for larger, higher-inertia arms.

Two hands, one arm

Both operator hands drive one arm-hand system. Single-operator bimanual control would need each hand coupled to its own arm while its fingers are tracked.

Vision-based fingers

Finger control depends on monocular landmarks and morphology-dependent retargeting, with no distributed fingertip force feedback.

Scope of evaluation

Ten participants, five short tasks, one platform and one policy task. Remapping, latency and contact cues are not yet isolated.
